Brand · Web · E-commerce UX

GUAIRE

Brand and website for an Irish streetwear label about modern Irish life.

Role
Positioning, UX, visual and graphic design, front-end build
Year
2026
Tools
Photoshop, Illustrator, Claude Code, HTML, CSS, JS, Vercel
guaire.vercel.app ↗

Problem

Irish clothing is crowded with heritage, Gaeilge and county brands. GUAIRE needed its own territory, and a pre-launch site that sells a feeling before anyone can buy a tee.

What I did

Mapped eight Irish competitors to find the gap: everyday modern Irish life. Structured the site as a day in that life (The Day, The Night, The Morning After), set a black-and-white system with small touches of green and gold, and designed the drop as a late-night chipper menu screen. Built four product pages with a back / front / both view switcher, plus reduced-motion and keyboard support throughout.

Designed the first run of graphic tees, each built on an everyday Irish reference: a road sign in Irish, a pint, a GAA pitch line, a 2000s mobile phone and the late-night shop sign. Then built photo-real front and back mockups in Photoshop, with worn print textures, to show the range as it would look for real.

Outcome

Live on Vercel with four product pages and a guest list for the Drop 01 launch, redesigned in fast iterations as the brand direction sharpened.

UX · Mobile app

Next Stop

A travel app that gives students everything they need for a trip in one place.

Role
UX research, wireframes, prototyping, UI
Context
Maynooth University
Tools
Paper, Figma

Problem

Students plan trips across a dozen apps and tabs. Next Stop pulls travel insights, activities and bookings into one flow.

What I did

Started with a rough paper prototype, took the feedback into a digital prototype in Figma, and checked every screen against usability principles:

  • User control and freedom: a bottom toolbar with Home as a clear emergency exit, and Back on every screen.
  • Aesthetic and minimalist design: cut the widgets from the paper version, used a light background with one marine-blue primary, and used figure-ground contrast to highlight the active element.
  • Consistency and standards: the same toolbar and logo placement on every page.
  • Fitts's and Miller's laws: large, reachable tap targets, and booking details chunked into small groups.

Outcome

A clickable Figma prototype covering the activity hub, booking flow and favourites.

Problem

Students want designer labels but can't pay full price, and most discount sites feel cheap. Verveza had to feel premium and affordable at once.

Process

How a project moves from question to live.

  1. 01

    Research

    Interviews, competitor maps and journey mapping to find the real problem and the red routes.

  2. 02

    Structure

    User flows and site maps before any pixels, so the hierarchy is settled early.

  3. 03

    Wireframe

    Paper first, then low-fidelity Figma frames to test layout cheaply.

  4. 04

    Prototype and test

    Clickable Figma prototypes, checked against Nielsen's heuristics, Fitts's and Miller's laws.

  5. 05

    Build and ship

    Responsive front-end with Claude Code, accessible by default, deployed and iterated.
